2024 Recap: Canada's AI Growth and Strategic Investments

Helen Oakley

As AI continues to evolve, Canada is emerging as a dynamic player in integrating this transformative technology. Larger enterprises and key sectors like finance, healthcare, and technology are leading the way, leveraging AI for fraud detection, personalized services, and operational efficiency. While industries such as manufacturing and retail face challenges like high costs, data privacy concerns, and talent shortages, mid-sized and smaller businesses are increasingly adopting AI. A 2024 Statistics Canada survey revealed that 6.1% of businesses were already using AI, with 10.6% planning adoption within the next year. Generative AI tools, such as ChatGPT, have also become prevalent, with 71% of professionals incorporating them into their work for applications like customer engagement, inventory management, and fraud detection. Despite these advancements, concerns over data privacy, AI inaccuracies, and governance highlight the need for thoughtful integration as the country balances opportunity with caution.

Government Investments in AI

Canada’s federal and provincial governments are driving AI innovation through significant investments aimed at fostering research, commercialization, and responsible integration of AI technologies. These efforts are not only enhancing AI capabilities across industries but also ensuring the ethical and secure use of these transformative tools.

At the federal level, the Pan-Canadian AI Strategy has been instrumental in positioning Canada as a leader in AI research and talent development. Launched in 2017 and renewed in 2022 with an additional $48 million in funding, the program focuses on advancing AI research and training through initiatives led by the Canadian Institute for Advanced Research (CIFAR). Learn more about the Pan-Canadian AI Strategy here.

The AI and Data Act (AIDA) is another key federal initiative under development, part of the Digital Charter Implementation Act. While still in progress, AIDA underscores the government’s commitment to regulating AI systems to protect Canadians and foster innovation responsibly. Learn more about the Digital Charter and AIDA here.

Provincially, investments are tailored to regional priorities:

  • Ontario has allocated $60 million through the Regional Artificial Intelligence Initiative (RAII) to help small and medium enterprises adopt and commercialize AI technologies, fostering innovation in areas like advanced manufacturing and logistics. Details on RAII can be found here.
  • Quebec is investing $217 million under the Stratégie québécoise de la recherche et de l'innovation (SQRI2) from 2022 to 2027. A notable $21 million has been directed to Mila, Quebec’s renowned AI research institute, which focuses on socially responsible AI and international collaborations. Learn more about Quebec’s innovation strategy here.
  • British Columbia is using its $32.2 million RAII funding to promote clean technology and sustainable innovation, aligning AI with environmental goals. More information about BC’s AI initiatives can be found here.
  • The Prairie Provinces (Alberta, Saskatchewan, and Manitoba) are leveraging $33.8 million through PrairiesCan to integrate AI into traditional sectors like agriculture and energy, fueling innovation in precision farming and energy efficiency. Explore PrairiesCan initiatives here.

These strategic investments are helping Canada advance its AI capabilities while addressing key challenges like sustainability, ethical governance, and workforce readiness. By focusing on both innovation and responsible adoption, Canada is positioning itself as a global leader in AI for years to come.

Frameworks and Canada's Regulatory Efforts

To address the challenges of AI adoption, frameworks like the AISUF Framework are providing essential guidance for Canadian businesses aiming for responsible integration. This framework emphasizes safety, transparency, and security, featuring a maturity grading system designed for both general AI applications and critical infrastructure. Globally, frameworks such as the U.S. NIST AI Risk Management Framework and the EU's AI Act showcase international efforts to establish trust, ethical standards, and transparency in AI use. Inspired by these global initiatives, Canada is making strides with its own regulatory efforts, including the anticipated Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA). While still in development, AIDA underscores Canada’s commitment to fostering responsible AI development and addressing national priorities effectively.

Looking Ahead: Canada’s AI Potential

Canada stands at a critical juncture in its AI journey, blending innovation with the careful consideration required for responsible integration. Larger enterprises and sectors like finance, healthcare, and technology are setting the pace, while smaller businesses and traditionally slower industries work to overcome obstacles like costs, data privacy, and workforce challenges.

Significant investments across Canada are bolstering these efforts, with federal initiatives such as the Pan-Canadian AI Strategy and provincial programs like Quebec’s innovation strategy and Ontario’s Regional Artificial Intelligence Initiative driving AI research, commercialization, and adoption. Frameworks like the AISUF Framework, emphasizing safety and transparency, and regulatory efforts such as the anticipated AI and Data Act (AIDA) further demonstrate Canada’s commitment to responsible innovation.

By addressing governance gaps, strengthening workforce readiness, and ensuring equitable distribution of investments, Canada has the opportunity to unlock AI’s full potential. With a focus on ethical and secure practices, the country is well-positioned to establish itself as a global leader in AI-powered innovation.
